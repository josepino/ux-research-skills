# Sources

The interview design is our own synthesis; it follows widely taught practice and the conventions of the skill set. Texts were paraphrased, not copied.

| Source | Contributes |
|---|---|
| Nielsen Norman Group, research planning and stakeholder articles https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ | Stage-based framing (discover / validate / post-launch), stakeholders and evidence |
| Interaction Design Foundation, What is UX Research https://www.interaction-design.org/literature/topics/ux-research | Process stages and research types behind the stage question |
| Alida, UX research guide https://www.alida.com/user-experience-research-guide | Pre-research and planning steps, ResearchOps context |
| Asana, Project Management Triangle https://asana.com/resources/project-management-triangle | Scope, cost and time constraints (Round 3) |
| GitLab Handbook, UX research https://handbook.gitlab.com/handbook/product/ux/ux-research/ | Example of team-level research practice and documentation |

## Our own heuristics (not external standards)
- Rounds of 1-3 questions, defaults and "Assumption" labels: a design choice to avoid interrogation fatigue.
- Under 80 lines and the three-month freshness rule: practical limits we set for the profile.
- Learner and consultant variants: our own adaptation to the two most common non-standard users.
