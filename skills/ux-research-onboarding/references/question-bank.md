# Question bank by round

Pick 1-3 questions per message. Skip anything already known. Offer an example or a default with each question. Mark unconfirmed guesses as "Assumption".

## Round 1: You
- What name should appear as the UX Researcher on your reports, and which organization or team (optional)? Ask this first; never guess it.
- What is your role, and how long have you been doing UX research (never, under 2 years, 2-5, 5+)? Default: intermediate.
- Who is on your team (designers, PMs, engineers, other researchers), and who do you work with most?
- How is research used today: ad hoc requests, regular cadence, only after launch, not at all?
- Has the team done research before? What worked and what did not?

## Round 2: Project
- What product or project is this for, and who is it for? One sentence is enough.
- What stage is it in: discover (finding problems), validate (testing ideas or prototypes), or post-launch (measuring and improving)? Default if unsure: validate.
- What decisions are pending that research could inform, and by when?
- Who are the users (segments, not names), and how easily can you reach them: easy, limited or none?
- What do you already have: analytics, support tickets, past studies, a prototype?

## Round 3: Constraints, stakeholders
- How much time do you have per study, and is there a budget for incentives or tools? Default: 2 weeks, no budget.
- Which tools do you already use (Figma, Google Forms, Zoom, Dovetail, Miro...)?
- Where do research notes and recordings live, if anywhere?
- Any privacy rules: recordings allowed, data location, legal review, consent templates? (Never paste real participant data.)
- Any accessibility needs for participants or for your deliverables?
- Who are the stakeholders, and what kind of evidence convinces them: numbers, video clips, quotes, a prototype demo?

## Round 4: Preferences
- Which language should deliverables be in, and who reads them (the `audience:` field)? Default: your language, product team.
- Preferred formats: one-page summary, slides, markdown, spreadsheet?
- Level of detail: brief, standard or deep? Tone: plain and direct, formal, playful?
- Learners: what do you want to learn first, and what would you do with it in your job?

## Round 5: Playback
- Summarize in under 15 lines. Ask: "What is wrong or missing?" and "Anything that must not go in the file?"
- Confirm the file name and location, then write and validate it.

## Learner mode (variant)
Use when the person is learning rather than running a study.
- What is your background (design, product, engineering, student, career change)?
- Do you have a real or practice project to apply it to? If not, offer a fictional one.
- What do you want to be able to do in 4 weeks (run an interview, analyze a survey, present findings)?
- How do you like to learn: explanations, exercises, quizzes, real cases?
- Set `mode: learner`, fill `learning_goals`, and route follow-up to `ux-research-coach`.

## Consultant mode (variant)
Use when the person works for several clients.
- How many clients or projects are active, and should each get its own profile? Default: one file per client folder.
- Who is the paying stakeholder versus the daily contact, and who reads reports?
- What are the contract limits: scope, number of rounds, data ownership, confidentiality, deliverable formats?
- Which of your own templates, tools and repository conventions should the skills respect?
- Set `mode: consultant`; never mix data from two clients in one profile.

## Defaults when the person does not know
| Unknown | Reasonable default (mark as Assumption) |
|---|---|
| Experience | intermediate |
| Stage | validate |
| Time | 2 weeks per study |
| Budget | none |
| Detail level | standard |
| Audience | product team |
