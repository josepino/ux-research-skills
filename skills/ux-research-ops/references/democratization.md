# Democratizing research

It happens when people who are not researchers (product, design, support, marketing) do research on their own. It can multiply learning, but without rules it produces biased studies, mishandled data and decisions built on weak evidence. Research does not become safe by being "everyone's": it needs clear limits.

## 1. Access and autonomy levels
Our proposal, adjustable to the team:
| Level | What they can do | Condition |
|---|---|---|
| 0. Consumer | Browse the repository and attend sessions as an observer | Basic confidentiality agreement |
| 1. Contributor | Take notes, observe, take part in the debrief | Mini observation training |
| 2. Guided autonomous | Low-risk conversations with existing customers, simple usability tests, short surveys | Plan reviewed by a researcher before it goes out |
| 3. Autonomous | Low-risk studies end to end | Track record of reviewed studies + after-the-fact review |
| Research | Medium- or high-risk studies, complex methods | Only researchers or under their supervision |

## 2. Minimum training
Before level 2: (a) ethics and consent (`ux-ethics-bias-review`), (b) unbiased questions and listening (`ux-interview-guide`), (c) how to handle data and name files (this skill), (d) writing a plan (`ux-research-plan`), (e) one accompanied practice: observe a session and then run one with feedback. Record who completed what. The team decides the duration.

## 3. What NOT to delegate
- **High risk**: minors, people in vulnerable situations, health, personal finances, legal topics.
- **Sensitive topics**: trauma, discrimination, special categories of data (health, orientation, beliefs), any topic where harm to the participant is possible.
- **Strategic decisions**: entering a market, pricing, shutting down a product, or decisions that are hard to reverse.
- Studies with statistical needs (formal quantitative sampling) and with complex accessibility.
- Any study with identifiable customer data that lacks a clear legal basis.
When in doubt, escalate (section 5).

## 4. Guardrails
1. Every study starts from a request or plan (`request-intake.md`), with a question and an associated decision.
2. **Peer review**: a more experienced person reads the plan, guide and consent before it goes out, and reviews findings and quotes before they are shared (`ux-insight-qa`).
3. Approved templates (consent, guide, notes) in the repository; they are not improvised.
4. Coordinated recruiting so the same people are not overloaded (`ux-participants-recruiting`).
5. Results go to the repository with method and limits declared ("when not to generalize").
6. Language rule: what comes out of a small study is presented as a hypothesis or signal, not as market truth.
7. Autonomy can be lost if rules are skipped: it is a conversation, not a punishment.

## 5. How to escalate doubts
A single channel (for example a chat channel or a form) with someone responsible for answering. Simple triage: is there risk to participants or an irreversible decision? → research takes it. Doubt about method? → review in a short meeting. Technical doubt? → template or guide. Log frequent questions to improve the training.

## 6. Warning signs
Studies nobody reviewed, quotes from one person presented as "the users", guides with questions that suggest the answer, recordings without consent, nobody knows what was studied before. Review them in the audit (`quarterly-audit.md`).
