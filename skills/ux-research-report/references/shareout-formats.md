# Shareout formats

The format decides whether findings are heard, discussed or archived. Choose by the **decision to be made** and by **how the audience works**, not by habit.

## Contents
1. Decision table · 2. How to prepare each format · 3. Combining formats · 4. Mistakes

## 1. Decision table
| Format | Best when | Typical length | Risk | Evidence it carries |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Live / in the room** | A decision is pending and a small group can discuss it; you want findings to "stick" | 30-45 min (heuristic) | Only those present hear it | Clips, quotes, printed journey |
| **Remote, synchronous** | Distributed team that must decide on the spot | 30 min (heuristic) + chat for questions | Cameras off, little discussion | Short slides, clips under one minute, shared document |
| **Workshop** | The next step is unclear, or the team must own the findings (prioritize, ideate) | 60-120 min (heuristic) | Ends with no decisions without facilitation | Short summary + working material (cards, "how might we...") |
| **Video clip / curated reel** | You need to build empathy or convince someone who will not read the report | 2-5 min | Taken out of context; exposes participants | Short moments with subtitles and consent for that use |
| **Newsletter / Slack / email** | Announce that the study is done, or low-effort follow-up | 1-2 min read | Skimmed, triggers no action | 5-line topline + link to the report |
| **Report / repository** | Future reference, audit, teams distant in time | Asynchronous reading | Almost nobody reads it whole | Everything, with a summary on top |
The sources agree that combining works best: a detailed report plus a presentation, and several formats for several audiences. A curated clip of a few minutes often persuades more than a long document (rampstackco, communication kit; practitioner opinion, not a measurement).

## 2. How to prepare each format
- **Live/remote:** state the meeting goal at the start ("by the end we decide X"); 3-4 main ideas; reserve discussion time; rehearse with an outsider; decide beforehand when questions are taken.
- **Workshop:** send the topline beforehand; in the workshop, only 10 minutes of context and the rest as activity (prioritize problems, write HMWs, vote). Mandatory output: decisions, owners and dates.
- **Clip:** 3-5 moments, each with one line of context and the "X of N" number showing it is not an isolated case; add subtitles; respect consent and pseudonymize (`ux-ethics-bias-review`); ask for confirmation before sharing outside the team.
- **Newsletter/Slack:** fixed structure: what we studied, 3 findings, what we recommend, link, who to ask. Template in `report-templates.md`.

## 3. Combining formats (sequences that work)
- Important decision: Slack topline -> short session with clips -> report in the repository.
- Discovery study: prioritization workshop a week after the report, with the supporting reel.
- Remote team: recorded walkthrough (5-10 min) + document + question thread.

## 4. Mistakes
- One format for everything.
- A workshop with no facilitator or defined outcome.
- A clip with the whole recording, or with faces and voices without permission.
- Presenting without saying which decision is expected.
After the shareout, measure whether anyone acted with `ux-research-impact`.
