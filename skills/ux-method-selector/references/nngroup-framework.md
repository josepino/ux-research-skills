# 3-dimension + stage framework (NN/g, Rohrer) and method map

Contents: 1. Dimensions · 2. Stage (generative/formative/summative) · 3. The 20 methods in one line · 4. Mixed methods · 5. Contradictions

## 1. Dimensions
- **Attitudinal vs behavioral** ("what they say" vs "what they do"; they often differ). Purely attitudinal: surveys, focus groups, card sorting. Purely behavioral: A/B testing, eye tracking. In the middle: usability tests and field studies (best tilted toward the behavioral).
- **Qualitative vs quantitative**: qualitative = directly observed/listened to (you can ask follow-ups and adjust the protocol; non-mathematical analysis). Quantitative = measured with an instrument (survey, analytics); the data is predefined. Qualitative answers *why/how to fix it*; quantitative *how many/how much* and helps prioritize. Card sorting, concept testing and desirability have qualitative and quantitative versions.
- **Context of use**: natural (more external validity, less control: field, intercept surveys, data mining) · scripted (focuses on a flow; the benchmark is the most rigid) · limited (participatory design, concept testing, card sorting, tree testing) · no product (brand, aesthetic attributes).

## 2. Stage
| Stage | Goal | Type | Typical methods |
|---|---|---|---|
| Strategize | Find directions/opportunities | Generative | field, diaries, interviews, surveys, participatory design, concept testing |
| Design | Improve usability | Formative | card sorting, tree testing, usability (moderated and unmoderated, remote) |
| Launch and assess | Measure against itself or competition | Summative | usability benchmark, unmoderated test, A/B, analytics/clickstream, surveys |

## 3. The 20 methods (one line)
Lab usability testing · field studies · contextual inquiry (like field, for complex systems) · participatory design · focus groups (3-12 people; of little use for usability, useful for first impressions of brand/concept) · interviews · eye tracking · usability benchmark (scripted, large samples) · moderated remote test · unmoderated test (a tool captures success, time, perceived ease) · concept testing · diary · customer feedback (self-selected sample) · desirability (associating attributes from a closed list with each visual alternative) · card sorting · tree testing · analytics/clickstream analysis · A/B · surveys · expert content/IA evaluation.
Key idea: almost every project benefits from several methods; many teams use only one or two out of habit.

## 4. Mixed methods (Maze)
Use them when neither qualitative nor quantitative alone answers. Designs: *convergent parallel* (both at once, analyzed separately and merged), *explanatory* (numbers first, then interviews to explain them), *exploratory* (qualitative first with few people, then quantitative to size it). Advantage: triangulation; cost: more time and risk of contradictory results that force more research.

## 5. Contradictions between sources
- Qualitative sample size: Maze attributes "40 participants" to NN/g for qualitative; Contentsquare and NN/g speak of 40+ for **quantitative/benchmark**. The skill uses 5 per round for qualitative and ~40 for quantitative; Maze seems to mix them up.
- Eye tracking: IxDF says <=5 participants; Smashing/Oculid says >=30 if only heatmaps are analyzed and >=6 if replays with think-aloud are viewed. See `methods/eye-tracking.md`.
