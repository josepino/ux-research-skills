# JTBD and switch interviews

Jobs to Be Done (JTBD) looks at the "job" a person hires a product to do in a specific situation. Switch interviews reconstruct the timeline of a real decision to switch (or not to switch). Origins associated with Clayton Christensen and Bob Moesta. Summary based on secondary sources (see `../sources.md`), not on the original books.

**When to use it**: understand why people adopt, abandon or switch products; discover real competitors (including the "do it by hand" solution); inform positioning and onboarding.
**When NOT**: testing usability (-> `ux-usability-testing`); measuring how many switch (-> `ux-survey-builder` or analytics); if nobody switched recently (memory fails with old events).

**Steps**
1. Define which switch you are studying (e.g. "moved from a spreadsheet to our app") and recruit people who did it **recently**; add people who evaluated and chose another option or stayed with what they had (`ux-participants-recruiting`).
2. Timeline guide: first thought of switching, the event that triggered it, alternatives looked at, doubts, the moment of deciding, first uses. Ask for concrete scenes ("tell me about that day"), not opinions (`ux-interview-guide`).
3. Code each interview according to the four usual JTBD forces: push (what bothered them), pull (what attracted them), anxiety (fears about the new thing) and habit (inertia). It is a useful simplification, not a law.
4. Write the job as "When [situation], I want [motivation], so I can [outcome]".
5. Look for patterns across interviews and map them to product decisions (`ux-synthesis`).

**Mistakes**: asking "what would you want?"; interviewing only fans; skipping the timeline; confusing the job with a feature; mixing in Ulwick's quantitative approach (outcome-driven) without saying so.

**Sample**: there is no reliable figure in the sources we reviewed; plan in rounds and stop when patterns repeat. Do not invent one.

Sources: web search of summaries (thoughtbot, jobstobedone.org, JTBD interview guides), all secondary; verify with *Competing Against Luck* (Christensen et al.).
