# Usability / UX benchmark (NN/g, 7 steps)

**When**: summative stage; track progress between versions, compare with a competitor, an industry standard or a business goal, and demonstrate the value of UX work. It is a **repeated program**, not a one-off study.

**Steps**
1. Choose what to measure: product, user group and ~5-10 main tasks. Choose 2-4 lasting metrics, aligned with KPIs. HEART (Google) organizes options: Happiness (satisfaction, ease, NPS), Engagement, Adoption, Retention, Task success (success, errors, time on task). Note: more time can be good (engagement) or bad (efficiency).
2. Choose how to measure: quantitative usability test, analytics and/or surveys (ideal: survey + behavioral method). Do not use a technique without the skills (better no numbers than bad numbers) nor one so expensive that it cannot be repeated. Review existing data first. With competitors you have no analytics of your own.
3. Baseline: **pilot it first** (you will probably discard that data); control external factors (campaigns, economy). A single measurement says nothing: compare with a competitor, an industry benchmark or a stakeholder goal.
4. Redesign. 5. Measure again with the same methodology. 6. Interpret (significance, not just numerical differences). 7. Optional: ROI.

**Sample**: relatively large samples, 40 or more (Contentsquare) in quantitative studies.
**Reference**: average NPS of hotel sites = 13% according to NN/g (industry data; verify it is current).
**Deliverable**: metrics dashboard per version with confidence intervals, documented methodology so it can be repeated.
