# Eye tracking

**When**: know where people look and for how long (visual attention), detect ignored elements, compare visual variants, hierarchy on a screen. It captures what the person does not remember or describe. Behavioral method; quantitative or qualitative depending on use.

**What it does NOT say**: why they look (only location/duration), whether they perceived what they looked at, peripheral vision, and its accuracy varies with glasses/pupil (IxDF). Combine it with think-aloud, surveys or clicks.

**Concepts**: AOI (area of interest), fixation, saccade. A long time to first fixation on an AOI (>0.15 s in the Smashing example) may indicate poor placement; a long gaze may be interest or confusion.

**Steps**: question and hypothesis -> metrics and AOIs -> tasks and stimuli -> lab or remote (moderated/unmoderated) -> recruit with diversity (do they wear glasses?) -> pilot the equipment -> session in a quiet environment, instructions off screen, real data in forms -> analyze gaze plots, heatmaps, replays -> cross with other methods.

**Sample (sources in tension!)**: IxDF recommends 5 or fewer to detect most problems; Smashing/Oculid: **>=30** if the analysis is only heatmaps and **>=6** if replays with think-aloud are viewed. Rule: qualitative = few; aggregated heatmaps = 30+.

**Cost/tools**: glasses such as Tobii Pro Glasses 3 for real environments (classroom, store, street); software using a webcam/front camera can be up to 100x cheaper (vendor claim, take with caution). Cheap alternative: click maps and recordings.
**Deliverable**: heatmaps, gaze plots, metrics per AOI, findings with recommendations.
