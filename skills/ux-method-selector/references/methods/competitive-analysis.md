# Competitive analysis

**What it is**: gathering and comparing data on products and companies in the market to see strengths and weaknesses and decide strategy (Dalrymple, 2018). Early/discovery stage; low-medium effort. It is desk research, not research with users.

**What it can include**: overview (products, companies, prices, market share), user demographics, feature list, social media presence, visual language, voice/tone and content.

**What it is for**: understanding the ground where you will compete, contrasting what is distinctive about your product, identifying possible user types, and comparing visual and language styles.

**Limits (what it does NOT say)**
- It says *what exists*, not *why*: a long feature list does not reveal which ones people value and use.
- There is no analytics from the competitor; to know how it performs in usability you must test it with users (benchmark, see `usability-benchmark.md`).

**Good practices**: include direct AND indirect competitors; keep it as a living document and update it; complement it with primary research (talk to users who use other products today to learn what they use and why).
**Deliverables**: comparison matrix, positioning map, gaps/opportunities.
**Sample**: not applicable (no participants); define a criterion for which competitors are included.
Sources: Dalrymple (Medium, 2018, short 2-minute note), Lyssna. Template details are missing; no steps are invented.
