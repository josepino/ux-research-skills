# Diary study

**When**: understand habits, routines and touchpoints over time, in real context; only works for what the participant can record easily. Generative stage (start). It typically lasts a couple of weeks (Maze).

**Design decisions**
- Format: free (more freedom, risk of losing data) or structured (fixed questions); digital or paper; photos, audio, notes.
- **Trigger**: by interval (one entry per day/week), by signal (the researcher pings) or by event (every time X happens).
- Frequency and load: the heavier the task, the more dropout.

**Steps**: define the question and the event to record -> choose format and trigger -> pilot -> recruit -> onboarding with examples -> light follow-up (reminders) -> closing interview about the entries -> thematic analysis.

**Main limit**: it depends on the participants' motivation and consistency; over-recruit because of dropout (no figure in the sources).
**Deliverables**: usage timeline, themes, quotes/photos, opportunities.

## Cultural probes and diary: reinforcement (think moto / UsabilityGeek, 2019)
- One source describes cultural probes as what is often called a diary study: participants document and reflect on aspects of their lives over a period, early in design. Because they record their own behavior, the view is more personal and less biased than observation or a one-off interview. This is a characterization from a single source.
- Case: 12 participants, 1 week, activity cards (increasingly deep questions about relationships with brands and people, routines and expectations, not only the typical food or transport). Playful materials (lottery tickets, stickers) were added to keep motivation; an analog kit on purpose, with no digital notifications. Recruited a week in advance, balancing age and place of residence; the kit was tested in several rounds with colleagues and friends until only a short intro was needed.
- Flow: probe -> group information and patterns -> in-depth interviews to validate and go deeper. Benefits: reflection before the interview, a personal and trusting setting, concrete starting points for conversation.
- When to prefer probes/diary: emotional exploration and early inspiration, habits over time. When not: if what matters is not easy for the participant to record.
- Complement (Hall, 2017): it often follows a contextual inquiry or interview with the most engaged participants; ask for photos or a scrapbook, clear instructions and periodic follow-ups to sustain commitment.
- Sample and dropout: the case used 12 people for 1 week (a single example, not a rule; no dropout figure).
- Contrast with the above: the diary "typically lasts a couple of weeks" (Maze); the probe case lasted 1 week. It does not contradict: duration depends on the question.
