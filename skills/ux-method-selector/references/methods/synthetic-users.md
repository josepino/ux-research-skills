# Synthetic users (AI-generated participants)

**Rule: they are only for early ideation and preparation; never to decide.** NN/g (2024) concluded that they do not replace research with real users and that relying on them risks wrong decisions.

**When it helps (with human review)**
- Preparing a study: exploring topics, rehearsing an interview guide.
- Summarizing existing information about a broad, well-documented group.
- Generating hypotheses or proto-personas that will be tested with real people.

**When NOT**
- Validating concepts: they tend to agree with the idea and give favorable, vague feedback.
- Behavioral questions: they exaggerate the positive (for example, they say they finished courses that real users abandoned).
- Prioritizing features or creating personas: they put many needs at the same level and without depth.
- Niche or specialized populations.
- Presenting them as findings from real users: NN/g considers this unethical.

**Safe steps**
1. Label everything generated as "synthetic / hypothesis".
2. Use it to extract questions and assumptions, not answers.
3. Run each hypothesis through the assumption map (`ux-research-plan`) and test it with real participants.
4. Record in the report that no synthetic evidence went into the conclusions (`ux-ethics-bias-review`).

**Sample**: not applicable; there is no number of "synthetic users" that validates a decision.

Source: NN/g, "Synthetic Users: If, When, and How to Use AI-Generated Research", https://www.nngroup.com/articles/synthetic-users/ (read in 2026: 3 earlier studies recreated with Synthetic Users and ChatGPT; focus on interviews).
