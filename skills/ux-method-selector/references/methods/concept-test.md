# Concept test

In UX this means showing the essence of a value proposition (description, sketch, storyboard or basic prototype) to see whether the problem and the idea resonate, before building. Rohrer (NN/g) places it among the methods of the strategy stage. Do not confuse it with the "technical proof of concept" (can it be built?), which belongs to engineering; here **desirability** is tested.

**When to use it**: new idea or big change, before designing in detail; to compare 2-3 concepts; to decide whether it is worth continuing.
**When NOT**: to know whether something is usable (-> `ux-usability-testing`); to predict sales or adoption; when the concept is already decided and only approval is sought.

**Steps**
1. Write the desirability hypothesis and the signal that would make you continue or stop (see `ux-research-plan`, assumption map).
2. Build the concept on 1 page or 1 screen, without distracting design decoration; show the problem it solves and how, without selling.
3. Recruit people who have the problem, not "anyone" (`ux-participants-recruiting`).
4. Show the concept and ask about their current situation first, then reactions: what they understand, what they would do, what worries them, what they use today. Avoid "would you use it?" (people tend to say yes).
5. Ask for a real commitment when possible (leave an email, join a waitlist, a trial payment) and weigh that above what is stated.
6. Synthesize by assumption: fulfilled / refuted / no evidence (`ux-synthesis`).

**Mistakes**: asking whether they like it; showing a single concept with no alternative; using friends or colleagues; confusing polite interest with demand; not defining beforehand which result means "do not continue".

**Sample**: none of our sources sets a figure for concept testing; do not invent one. For qualitative, follow the general rule of small rounds until findings repeat, and justify it in the plan.

**Deliverable**: sheet with concept, hypothesis, evidence per assumption, decision (continue, pivot, stop) and next test.

Source: NN/g, "When to Use Which UX Research Methods", https://www.nngroup.com/articles/which-ux-research-methods/ (already in `../sources.md`).
