# Desirability, competitive analysis, content audit, web analytics (limited sources)

The assigned pages give little detail on these; whatever is unsupported is marked. Complement with future sources.

## Desirability study
Visual alternatives are shown and each person associates them with attributes from a **closed list** (NN/g); it is useful for brand tone/aesthetics and does not require using the product. It can be qualitative or quantitative. Quantitative: count attributes per alternative. If you use scales, prefer balanced ones (equal number of positive and negative options, with a neutral point): removing the midpoint distorts and favors social-desirability bias (Data Force).

## Competitive analysis
It now has its own file: `competitive-analysis.md`. Summary: review competitors' features, usability and positioning, reviews and feedback to find gaps (Lyssna, discovery). For a comparative benchmark you need to test the competitors with users: you do not have their analytics (NN/g).

## Content audit/evaluation
The sources only name it as an expert method; no steps. Suggestion without source support: inventory + quality criteria.

## Web analytics / clickstream
Behavioral, quantitative, natural; useful in the summative stage and for benchmarks (abandonment rate, adoption, conversion). Heatmaps and session replay allow a qualitative reading (Contentsquare). It says *what* happens, not *why*; add in-app surveys or interviews. Beware of external factors (campaigns) when attributing changes.

## Concept testing, five-second test, focus groups
Concept testing: presenting the essence of the value proposition to few or many people (Rohrer). Five-second: immediate impressions, attitudinal, from ideation on. Focus groups 3-12 people; of little use for usability.

## Discovery (Lyssna)
Mix stakeholder interviews (vision, KPIs, constraints), competitors, interviews, surveys, contextual inquiry and analytics; it is continuous, not only at the start.
