# Tool catalog by stage

Descriptions come from official pages or the guides cited (see `sources.md`). Plans and prices change: verify before recommending. The tool matters less than rigor: a card sort in a spreadsheet that is analyzed well is worth more than one in an expensive platform that is misinterpreted (Butterfly).

## 1. Criteria for choosing
The MGH librarians' guide rates each tool on four axes: time, cost, required knowledge and user commitment. Use those four axes with the learner, plus "does it handle personal data and where is it stored?" (privacy). Also the project management iron triangle (Asana): scope, cost and time are linked; if the learner wants more scope with less time, something gives (quality or cost).

## 2. Plan and collaborate
- **Figma / FigJam**: collaborative canvas for prototypes and whiteboards (maps, affinity). Prototypes for testing.
- **Miro / FigJam / Google Docs**: parking lot and live workshops (NN/g).
- **Google Workspace**: shared documents, sheets and slides for plans, scripts and findings tables.

## 3. Recruit and survey
- **Google Forms**: simple surveys and screeners, with conditional logic and templates; free to start. No advanced analytics.
- **Qualtrics**: experience management (XM) platform for surveys and corporate programs; enterprise oriented.
- **Maze**: research and testing platform; offers a recruiting panel (the website cites more than 6 million participants), qualitative and quantitative studies, automatic reports and AI assistance.
- Own recruiting: see skill `ux-participants-recruiting`.

## 4. Test and observe
- **Maze**: prototype tests and information architecture tests (the Butterfly guide also places it for card sorting).
- **Optimal Workshop**: card sorting (cited by Butterfly as a freemium option).
- **UserTesting, Lookback**: usability tests (cited by Butterfly). For test design see `ux-usability-testing`.
- **Zoom / Teams**: remote interviews.
- **Delta testing (Centercode)**: "real world" beta tests with automated feedback and surveys (the source is a vendor glossary).

## 5. Analytics and behavior
- **Hotjar (now Contentsquare)**: the official site says Hotjar no longer accepts new accounts and is migrating to Contentsquare Free: heatmaps and attention maps, session recordings, surveys, funnels, error and performance monitoring. In the free plan they state 200k sessions per month versus 35 per day on Hotjar Free. Commercial claim, verify it.
- **Microsoft Clarity**, **GA4**: free analytics and recordings (cited by Butterfly).
- They tell you "what" happens; for the "why" combine with interviews or tests (LogRocket).

## 6. Notes, synthesis and repository
- **Obsidian**: local, private, linked notes; useful for a personal repository. It is a notes app, not a UX tool.
- **Dovetail**: transcription, tagging, summaries (cited by NN/g and GitLab).
- **Nextcloud**: open-source collaboration and file platform on a private cloud; an option when participant data cannot leave for third-party services.
- Naming conventions and repository: skill `ux-research-ops`. Signal from Dscout: few companies have a repository that works without a dedicated ResearchOps person.

## 7. AI for research
See `ai-in-ux-research.md`. Usage summary: transcribe and summarize (Dovetail, Otter.ai), interview at scale (Marvin, UserFlix, Outset), find sources (Perplexity, ScholarAI), cluster notes (Miro), summarize heatmaps (Contentsquare Sense AI). Looppanel lists 10 AI tools (vendor article, read critically).

## 8. Remote and asynchronous collaboration
Virtual sticky notes and asynchronous work serve distributed teams: document so others can decide without a meeting, and use parking lots with an owner and date. The Krisp, Stormboard and Blakiston pages are blog/marketing material; they add little rigor.
