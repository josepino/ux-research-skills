# Sparring question bank

When to read: in mode 5. Pick 5-8 from the stage; ask one at a time and push back where the answer is weak. These are questions to think with, not an exam.

## Plan
1. What concrete decision will change depending on what you learn? Who makes it and when?
2. What do you believe today and what evidence would change your mind?
3. Is this question answered by this method, or is there a cheaper one?
4. What does the organization already know (prior studies, data) that does not need repeating?
5. What happens if the results contradict what the team wants to hear?
6. What is the minimum scope that would help and what are you leaving out on purpose?
7. How much time and budget do stakeholders really have to act?

## Recruiting
1. Who is the person who best answers your question and how are they different from the one who is easy to get?
2. How do you avoid recruiting only people who already like you (enthusiastic customers, the usual panel)?
3. Which screener filters exclude someone relevant without you noticing?
4. How many people per segment and why that number? What would you do if a segment does not fill?
5. How do you know the people who responded fit the profile (verification, attention checks)?
6. What incentive do you offer and how can it bias who takes part?
7. Are there people with accessibility needs that your process excludes?

## Analysis
1. How many people support this theme? Does anyone contradict it?
2. Did they say it or do it? If there is a difference, which weighs more and why?
3. What evidence would have refuted this interpretation and did you look for it?
4. Does this quote represent the pattern or is it the most memorable?
5. Who else coded this? Do you agree?
6. Which part of the finding is what you saw and which is what you think?
7. If the team already believed this, how do you know you are not just confirming it?
8. Which data fit no theme and what do they tell you?

## Report
1. If they only read the first paragraph, what decision would they make?
2. Which recommendation has no finding behind it?
3. Which limits of the study could be used to dismiss your conclusions, and did you state them first?
4. What would the most skeptical person in the room ask you?
5. What do you show in 5 minutes and what stays in the appendix?
6. Is there language that sounds like certainty and is a hypothesis?
7. Who needs this in a format other than the report?

## Impact
1. Who owns acting on each recommendation? Do they know?
2. What changed (or did not) the last time something similar was presented?
3. How will you know, three months from now, whether the recommendation worked?
4. Which business or product metric moves and how do you connect it without exaggerating?
5. What happens if nothing is done? Is it costly or tolerable?
6. What is the smallest next step someone can take this week?
7. How do you record the decision so it does not get lost?
