## 1. Does it need an incentive?
- HubSpot: the safest way not to bias is to give no incentive; if the response rate is already good, you do not need one. Five-step decision: do you need one?, type, value, delivery, timing.
- Survicate: incentives raise response by roughly 10-30% depending on audience and type, but can attract reward seekers; for short surveys needing fast volume, a guaranteed incentive works. (Blog figure, not a study.)
- Figures quoted by blogs (not verified by us): a USD 5 prepayment doubled responses; a US Department of Veterans Affairs study reported +30% with USD 5 (HubSpot); +19% (Lyssna). Use them only as an order of magnitude.
- Promised incentives (announced at the start, delivered at the end) help with long forms; prepaid ones work differently (HubSpot).

## 2. Types
- Cash or equivalent (gift cards) is the most used and effective; immediate delivery works better than a future promise (Tremendous). Gift cards are the most common UX incentive (Lyssna).
- Your own products, trials or early access **bias** toward the favorable, because people who want them respond more (Lyssna).
- B2B: professional value (reports, early access, a donation in their name); many people cannot accept money because of company policy (Survicate).
- Prize draws: cheaper; a meaningful but not disproportionate prize (Vantage Circle suggests USD 50-100 for internal surveys).
- Internal or short customer surveys: transparency and showing what changed thanks to previous feedback can beat monetary rewards (Vantage Circle).
- Table: 2-5 min no money; 5-10 min optional token USD 5-10; 10-20 min draw USD 25-50; 20-45 min gift card USD 15-25; 45+ min direct payment USD 25-50+ (Vantage Circle, internal surveys; starting points only).

## 3. Reference amounts for external research
| Format | Range | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Short survey 5-10 min | USD 5-15 | Lyssna (2026) |
| Long survey 15-30 min | USD 15-40 (or 10-40 depending on complexity) | Lyssna |
| Unmoderated test 15-30 min | USD 20-50 | Lyssna |
| Moderated session 60 min | USD 75-150 (more for specialists) | Lyssna |
| Moderated consumer sessions, remote | USD 60-100/hour; USD 80/hour recommended as a balance between completed sessions, fill rate and no-shows | User Interviews (25,000+ sessions) |
- At USD 100/hour no-shows were lowest (6-7%); at USD 60/hour about 10% (User Interviews).
- Professionals and markets with different living costs need adjustment (Lyssna).
- Payments may be taxable income depending on jurisdiction (Tremendous, US-focused); check local rules.

## 4. Bias risks
- An incentive that is very high relative to duration attracts rushers (satisficing) or people who lie about eligibility (Vantage Circle, Tremendous).
- Qualtrics: limit the survey to the target audience and choose something valuable for that audience; it does not motivate someone who does not want the product.
- Offer a range of rewards with a value cap so each person can choose (Tremendous).
- Get informed consent and define payment frequency and distribution (Tremendous).
- Quality controls: minimum time, verification questions, review of nonsense open answers (SmartSurvey: clean before analyzing).

## 5. Screeners (to recruit respondents)
- Purpose: let the right people "in" and the wrong ones "out"; define inclusion and exclusion criteria beforehand, and which to relax if recruiting is hard (Lyssna).
- 5-10 questions, at most 10 (Participant Kit, Lyssna). Funnel from general to specific (PlaytestCloud).
- Neutral questions; options that cover everything without overlap, with "none / not applicable / other"; avoid yes/no questions that reveal the "right" answer (Participant Kit, PlaytestCloud, Lyssna).
- Recent-behavior questions (e.g. "did you buy X in the last 3 months") and exclusion of people who work for competitors (Participant Kit).
- A first open question helps to see how the person expresses themselves (PlaytestCloud).
- Branching or skip logic; make incentive, duration and next steps clear (Lyssna).
- Accessibility and informed consent apply to the screener too (Participant Kit).
- Pilot the screener. For full recruiting, see the `ux-participants-recruiting` skill.
