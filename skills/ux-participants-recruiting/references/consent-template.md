# Informed consent template (adapt to the case and to local regulation)

A Spanish copy for Spanish-speaking researchers is in `consent-template.es.md`; give participants the consent form in their own language. Have a lawyer or the person responsible review it before first use.

```markdown
# Consent to take part in a user research study

**Organization/team:** …  **Person responsible:** …  **Contact:** …

1. **Purpose of the study.** We want to understand [goal, in simple language]. We are not evaluating your abilities: we are evaluating the product.
2. **What we will do.** [Interview/tasks on a prototype/survey] of about [N] minutes, [online/in person].
3. **Recording.** With your permission we will record [audio/video/screen] only to analyze the session. ☐ I accept recording  ☐ I do not accept (we will take notes).
4. **Data we collect.** [Answers, observations, contact details, etc.]. We do not collect [sensitive data that is not needed].
5. **Who will have access and how it is protected.** Only the research team [and …]. Reports will use pseudonyms or aggregated data. Data is stored in [place] for [period]; afterwards it is deleted.
6. **Use of quotes or images.** ☐ I accept anonymous quotes in internal reports  ☐ …in public materials.
7. **Voluntary participation.** You can skip questions, pause or withdraw at any time without consequences. If you withdraw, you can ask us to delete your data.
8. **Incentive.** You will receive [incentive] [even if you withdraw].
9. **Risks and benefits.** [Minimal expected risks; if the topic is sensitive, say so and offer a pause/support].
10. **Questions.** You can write to [contact].

**Statement:** I have read and understood this information and agree to take part.
Name: ________  Date: ________  Signature/electronic acceptance: ________
```

Notes:
- Simple language; offer an accessible version (easy read, screen reader, read aloud).
- Do not hide the company or the goal "to avoid bias": it reduces trust and weakens consent. If you must conceal something for validity, explain it and reveal it in the debrief.
- For minors, people with restricted legal capacity, or legal/medical topics: consult legal counsel or the people responsible.

## Added good practices (NN/g, "Obtaining Consent for User Research" and its sample form)
- **Modular consent**: separate checkboxes (take part / record audio / record video / let others observe / use data in reports or training). Someone who refuses recording can still take part, which raises participation.
- **Concrete purpose, without revealing the hypothesis**: not as vague as "get to know our customers". State duration and a defined end; avoid open-ended "umbrella" consents.
- **Data**: is it anonymized? Who has access? Is it published? When is raw material deleted? Can the person ask for deletion? Contact of the person responsible.
- **Compensation**: make clear it does not depend on performing well or giving positive comments; in person, pay right after signing.
- **Minors and adults with a guardian**: a signature block for the parent/guardian; see also NN/g's article on consent vs assent with minors.
- **Information sheet or debrief** at the end if you must explain more of the purpose without influencing behavior; **always give a copy** of the signed consent.
- **When a full form is not needed**: A/B tests (routine product use, aggregated data) and intercept studies (a brief agreement page is enough; in private places, the establishment's permission). An informal call with a customer stops being informal when you transcribe quotes or share their data: then you need consent.
- **Before the session**: send the form in advance in an accessible format so they can read it without pressure.
- **Sensitive content**: warn from the screener and allow skipping questions.
