# Incentives: how much, what kind and how to pay

Contents: 1. Principles · 2. Types · 3. Indicative ranges · 4. User Interviews data · 5. Common mistakes

The figures come from US/Canada platforms in dollars and from commercial providers (Lyssna, User Interviews): they are **starting points, not universal rates**. Adjust for local cost of living and convert currency before fixing the amount.

## 1. Principles
- An incentive recognizes time and expertise; it is not a bribe. It becomes coercive when it is so high that someone takes part even when uncomfortable or when they do not fit the profile (Lyssna).
- Raise it gradually if recruiting stalls: it is usually a sign the amount is low for the effort asked.
- Proportional to: duration, profile expertise (executives/specialists more), recruiting difficulty, in person vs remote (in person pays more: travel), and market/location.
- Pay on time, also if the session goes badly or the person withdraws. If you ask for a purchase for the study, advance the money or reimburse quickly (Dscout inclusion guide).
- Offer alternatives (a donation to a nonprofit as an option, never as the only one).
- With your own customers money is sometimes not needed (early access, influence on the product), but some recognition is still advisable (User Interviews).
- Your own products/discounts can bias toward more favorable opinions (Lyssna). For employees of other companies, check gift policies.
- State it in the consent form: compensation does not depend on performing well or giving positive feedback; in person, pay at the start, right after signing (NN/g).

## 2. Types
Cash/transfer (maximum flexibility; may complicate accounting), gift cards (the most common; in international studies verify they work in the country), product access/discounts, donation, and non-monetary (recognition, content) that only work with high intrinsic motivation.

## 3. Indicative ranges by method (Lyssna, USD)
| Method | Typical duration | Range |
|---|---|---|
| Short survey | 5-10 min | 5-15 |
| Long survey | 15-30 min | 15-40 |
| Unmoderated usability test | 15-30 min | 20-50 |
| Moderated usability test | 30-60 min | 50-100 |
| Interviews | 45-90 min | 75-150 |
| Diary | days/weeks | 100-300+ |
| Longitudinal | ongoing | 150-500+ |
Example from the same source: a 30-minute interview with a senior executive may justify 200-300; with general consumers, 50-75.

## 4. User Interviews data (25,000+ moderated sessions, US/Canada)
- Moderated consumers: 74% of sessions paid between 60 and 100 USD/hour; most common, 100 USD/h. No-show: 10% at 60 USD/h, 8% at 80, 7% at 100.
- Remote: they recommend about 80 USD/h (no-show 8%). In person: most common 100 USD/h (42% of sessions).
- Under 40 USD/h is only 7% of completed sessions and is associated with more no-shows.
- Their report also defines "fill rate" (completed sessions / requested) and no-show (absent / (absent + completed)): use them as metrics of your own recruiting process.
- Caveat: the source is a platform that sells recruiting.

## 5. Common mistakes
Paying late; an incentive that rewards only "positive" answers; the same amount for very different profiles; ignoring the travel cost of participants with disabilities (for accessibility, offer to cover travel by the means they prefer, taxi included, and simplify the reimbursement process).
