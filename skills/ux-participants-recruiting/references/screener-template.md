# Screener template

```markdown
# Screener — [Study]
Introduction (do not reveal the exact goal):
"We are looking for people for a [duration] conversation about [general topic]. You will receive [incentive]. We will ask a few questions to see whether the study is a good fit for you. Your answers will only be used for this selection."

## Questions
| # | Question | Type | Rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | What do you do for a living? | Open | Disqualify if they work at [competitor/UX agency/company] |
| 2 | Which of these activities did you do in the last 3 months? (select all) [real 1] [distractor A] [distractor B] [real 2] [distractor C] | Multiple choice | Qualifies if they select [real 1] |
| 3 | How many times a month do you shop online? (0 / 1–2 / 3–5 / 6+) | Closed | Qualifies at 3+; quota by range |
| 4 | Tell me about the last time you [activity]. | Open | Assess clarity and detail |
| 5 | Which devices do you use for this? | Multiple | Needs [mobile/desktop] |
| 6 | Do you use any assistive technology (screen reader, magnifier, voice control, etc.)? Which one? | Open/multiple | Does not disqualify; informs adaptation |
| 7 | Do you have any needs to take part comfortably (schedule, format, accessibility)? | Open | Record |
| 8 | Which days/times could you do? | Calendar | |
| 9 | Contact details and age range | Fields | Only if they qualify |

## Quotas / diversity
Define how many per segment (age, experience, device, technical level, accessibility).

## Messages
- Accepted: confirmation with date, link, duration, attached consent, contact.
- Not selected: respectful thank-you, no details about the profile sought.
```

When you deliver a screener, translate the headings and field labels to the user's language.

## Worked example: behaviour frequency without revealing the target
Target: people who pay bills from the phone at least weekly.
- Closed list that gives it away (avoid): "How often do you pay bills from your phone? Weekly / Monthly / Never".
- Hidden version: "Which of these did you do in the last month? (select all) Paid a bill from the phone / Booked a medical appointment online / Ordered food / Renewed a subscription / Sent money to a friend / None of these". Then ask openly: "Tell me about the last time you paid for something from your phone." Qualifies if the answers include bill payment and the story shows recent, concrete use.

## Added good practices (see sources.md)
- **Order**: from broad to specific, with disqualifying questions first so you do not waste people's time. At most about 10 questions (advice from a practitioner cited by Lyssna).
- **Criteria**: prefer behaviors, experience and context of use (what they do, how often, where, how familiar) over demographics (IxDF, User Interviews). Recruit by behavior, not identity.
- **Neutrality**: replace "Do you play on mobile at least once a week?" (reveals the goal) with "What do you do in your free time in a typical week?" (IxDF). Include "not applicable" and "not sure" options.
- **Pilot**: test the screener with 2-3 people; adjust clarity, flow and timing.
- **Disqualified people**: thank them, do not reveal the profile sought and, if they agree, ask permission to keep their data for future studies.
- **Backups ("floaters")**: when selecting, add 1-2 people who almost fit to cover last-minute no-shows (IxDF).
- **Verification**: when confirming the session, re-check the key data (profile fit) and spot people who self-select for the incentive.
- **Welcome screen**: screener length, how data will be used and stored, how to withdraw, and when the chosen ones will be notified.
- **Video screener** (optional): ask for a short video in interviews where the ability to express oneself matters (Lyssna).
- **Accessible and ethical**: the screener itself must be accessible (contrast, keyboard), carry consent for processing the data, and comply with the privacy rules that apply (Participant Kit mentions GDPR/CPRA).
- **Accessibility question template**: "Do you use assistive technology? Which one (and which version)?" plus "Would you describe yourself as a person with a disability? No / Not sure / Yes / I prefer to write it".
