# Selling research: ROI and business case

When to read this file: the person must convince a boss/client/executive, asks to "justify", "budget", "ROI", or the plan needs a "Why now" section.

## Golden rule about figures
The figures below are quotes from industry blogs, many without verifiable methodology. Present them as "according to [source]" and as an order of magnitude, never as a promise. Whenever possible, replace them with the business's own data (cost of rework, support tickets, abandonment, development hours).

## Business case step by step
1. **Understand the resistance.** Ask skeptics what their barriers are (funding, time, doubts about the value) with real curiosity, as in an interview (UX Design Institute).
2. **Tie the value to money-making activities** (IxDF): purchasing/information, operations (less rework, fewer development "whys"), marketing and sales (a product with clear benefits), delivery and after-sales (fewer support calls).
3. **Compare scenarios.** Build two lines: (A) invest a little in research and change course in time; (B) skip it and discover months later that the feature did not solve the problem. The UX Design Institute uses an illustrative example of about 800 EUR and 2 extra weeks versus building the wrong thing.
4. **Propose a minimum viable research plan**: the best possible with minimum time/money (e.g. one extra week and a very small budget, per the same article). If it works, you come back with evidence to ask for more.
5. **Start small if there is wariness:** Boagworld suggests a survey or three interviews, and that the amount of research be proportional to the size and complexity of the project.
6. **Show existing research first:** support, sales, marketing and IT/analytics already have data; reviewing it first saves budget and reveals what is missing (Boagworld).
7. **Measure and tell the impact afterwards**, even with qualitative stories (User Interviews).

| Research-informed button change: +45% conversion, ~USD 15M in a month ("the 300 million dollar button" case, J. Spool) | Maze | Boagworld attributes "USD 300M extra" to Amazon: it is the same case told differently. Cite as an anecdote. |
| McAfee: -90% support costs after a redesign | Boagworld | Anecdote from a large company. |
| "At least 50% of development time is spent on avoidable rework" without research | Maze | Claim from a vendor, no methodology cited. Boagworld says almost the same ("avoids 50% wasted time"). Use as an argument, not as hard data. |

## Suggested structure for "Why now" (5 lines)
1. Decision being made and deadline.
2. Cost of being wrong (in own data: development hours, abandonment, tickets).
3. Cost of the study (minimum version and ideal version).
4. What would change depending on the result.
5. How impact will be measured.

## Common mistakes
- Arriving with statistics from giant companies to a small team: ask for something proportional.
- Presenting an exact ROI without own data.
- Not defining who will act on the results (the research ends up in a drawer).
- Equating research with usability testing: research includes audits, competitors, benchmarking and analogs (Openfield).
