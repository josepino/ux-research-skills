# Sharpening research questions

**When to read it**: the question is vague, a topic, or yes/no. A research question is useful when one possible answer changes a decision.

## Types of weak question
- **Topic**: names a theme ("onboarding", "pricing"), not something to learn.
- **Yes/no or taste**: "do they like X?". Polite answers do not move decisions.
- **Solution inside**: "how do we improve the chatbot?" presupposes the chatbot.
- **Too broad**: "get to know users".

## The decision-question test
1. Which decision does it inform and who makes it?
2. What possible answers are there and what would we do with each?
3. Can it be answered by dealing with real people (not only desk analysis)?
4. Is it open and specific about a context, moment or user?

## Before / after examples
| Before | After |
|---|---|
| "Do they like our onboarding?" | "At which onboarding step do new users hesitate about continuing, and what do they do then?" |
| "We want to understand the checkout" | "What do returning customers do and think between putting a product in the cart and abandoning, and what would make them complete the purchase?" |
| "Which features do we build?" | "What unsolved problems do store administrators have when closing the month, and how do they solve them today?" |
| "Why is conversion dropping?" | "What do people arriving at the pricing page expect to find, and how does it differ from what they see?" |
| "Is the new search usable?" | "Can new users find a specific product with the new search without help, and where do they get stuck?" -> goes to `ux-usability-testing` |
| "Are they interested in the annual plan?" | "Which situations led customers to switch or cancel their plan in the last three months?" |

(Examples written by us; inspired by the idea, used in several research guides, of moving from topic questions to decision questions.)

## Recipe
1. Write the decision: "We will decide [X] before [date]".
2. Write the requester's raw question.
3. Remove the solution and the adjectives ("better", "easy").
4. Add user + situation + behavior to observe.
5. Check it with the test above. If it has more than three questions, split it into studies or prioritize.
Then move from research question to interview questions with `ux-interview-guide`.
