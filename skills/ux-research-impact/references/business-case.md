# Business case for a study, tool or hire

When to read it: when you must ask for budget, time, a tool or a hire. It complements the ROI and business case reference of the `ux-research-plan` skill (cited figures and their caveats); here the document is assembled.

## Structure (one to two pages)
1. **Decision and deadline:** what will be decided and when.
2. **Problem and current evidence:** what we know today (support, analytics, sales data) and what we do not.
3. **Cost of being wrong:** in own data (development hours, abandonment, tickets). If there is none, use an own estimate with the assumptions in view.
4. **What is requested:** study, tool or hire, with concrete scope.
5. **Scenarios:** (A) invest a little and adjust in time; (B) do not research and discover late; (C) the ideal option. Show cost, timing, risk and what is learned in each.
6. **Minimum viable cost:** the smallest version that still answers the decision (for example, 5 sessions with users instead of a full program; the number depends on the method, see `ux-research-plan`).
7. **Success metrics:** leading and lagging, defined in advance (see `metrics-and-attribution.md`).
8. **Risks and limits:** what can go wrong, what the research will not solve.
9. **What would change depending on the result:** each possible result leads to an action.
10. **How it will be reported:** review dates and decision log.

## Depending on what is requested
- **A study:** focus on the decision and the cost of being wrong; minimum viable cost.
- **A tool:** compare against doing it by hand, include adoption time and who will use it; avoid justifying with vendors' "X% savings" that have no methodology.
- **A hire:** which decisions today are made without evidence, how much was outsourced, which roadmap studies are not done for lack of capacity. Ask first for a limited trial (for example, one quarter) if there is wariness.

## Figures: rules of use
- Priority 1: the business's own data.
- Priority 2: industry figures cited "according to [source]" and as an order of magnitude. Beware of the most repeated ones: the return range attributed to Forrester, the "50% avoidable rework", the "100 times more expensive after launch" and the button and McAfee cases have no verifiable methodology on the pages that cite them (see the table with caveats in the other file).
- Priority 3: own estimate, labeled and with assumptions.
- Never a point ROI without own data; offer ranges.
- Examples with amounts (such as the comparison of a small study versus building the wrong thing) are illustrative: say so.

## Resistance and tone
First ask what the decision maker's barriers are (budget, time, doubts about the value) and answer those. Show the existing research you already used. Start small and come back with evidence.

## Common mistakes
- Statistics of giants for a small team.
- Asking for "more research" without saying which decision it improves.
- Not showing what happens if the answer is "no".
- Promising an impact that is later not measured.
